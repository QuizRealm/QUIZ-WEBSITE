The Archives of Tartus & The Templar Coast

A historic coastal strip featuring pristine islands, ancient Phoenician ruins, and massive Crusader fortifications. Dive into the historical records of the Templar Coast below.

The Templar Coast & Crusader Fortifications

The coastal governorate of Tartus is historically celebrated as a cornerstone of the Crusader states, heavily shaped by military monastic orders like the Knights Templar and the Knights Hospitaller. Because this historic coastal strip acted as the primary maritime lifeline supplying European reinforcements and trade to the Kingdom of Jerusalem, it was heavily fortified with some of the most sophisticated military architecture ever conceived. Massive stone curtain walls, deep rock-cut ditches, and towering keep towers were constructed along the rocky shores to protect Christian shipping lanes from inland Muslim counter-offensives.

The city of Tartus itself (known to medieval Europeans as Tortosa) featured a magnificent cathedral dedicated to Our Lady of Tortosa, widely considered one of the finest achievements of medieval Gothic architecture in the entire Near East. The Templars utilized the city's coastal fortifications as an impregnable base, integrating naval supply lines directly with land defenses. Unlocking Tartus on your map connects you directly to this dramatic era of medieval military monasticism, where religion, commerce, and total fortification converged along the blue Mediterranean waves.

Arwad Island: The Only Syrian Island

Just a few kilometers off the coast of Tartus lies Arwad (ancient Arados), holding the unique geographical distinction of being the only inhabited island in all of Syria. Mentioned in ancient Egyptian Amarna letters and Assyrian royal inscriptions, Arwad was a fiercely independent Phoenician maritime city-state.

Protected by the natural sea barrier, its ancient Phoenician builders constructed massive sea walls of giant megalithic blocks to withstand winter storms and naval blockades. In medieval times, the Templars maintained Arwad as their final offshore stronghold long after mainland fortresses had fallen, before finally evacuating in 1302.

Margat Castle: Fortress in the Sky

Perched dramatically on a dark volcanic basalt ridge high above the coastal plain near Baniyas sits Margat Castle (Qal'at al-Marqab), famously known as the "Fortress in the Sky." Acquired by the Knights Hospitaller in 1186, its massive black basalt walls dominate the horizon.

Margat was so vast and heavily garrisoned that it could house a thousand soldiers alongside massive stables and administrative halls. Its sweeping round tower acted as an invincible artillery platform, allowing the Hospitallers to command absolute control over both land routes and coastal waters.

Ancient Phoenician Roots

Long before the arrival of the Templars and Crusaders, the coastal strip of Tartus was a thriving center of Phoenician civilization. Sites like Amrit (ancient Marathus) reveal stunning pre-classical architecture, including massive monumental rock-cut tombs (*megaliths*) and an open-air temple featuring a sacred water basin.

These ancient Phoenician engineers mastered coastal quarrying and maritime trade, establishing the foundational maritime infrastructure that would be utilized, adapted, and fought over by every subsequent empire for the next three thousand years.

The Maritime Significance of Tortosa

The historical significance of Tartus cannot be overstated. As the primary continental port facing Arwad Island, it formed a dual urban defense system where naval fleets could anchor safely behind natural reefs and island breakwaters. When the Crusader states were slowly compressed by Islamic armies under Zengi, Nur al-Din, and Baibars, Tartus acted as the ultimate northern refuge for retreating European military orders.

Today, the modern city of Tartus gracefully blends its medieval Crusader cathedral and ancient stone walls with vibrant Mediterranean seaside life, while Arwad Island remains a living museum where fishermen build wooden boats by hand in narrow alleys untouched by modern cars.
