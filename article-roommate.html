A Practical Roommate Conversation Checklist

Talk about shared space, schedules, guests, and small responsibilities before a minor mismatch becomes a recurring surprise.

A useful roommate conversation is about specific routines, not personality labels. Two people can enjoy different schedules and still share a space comfortably when expectations are clear. Use this checklist as a starting point, and write down the practical arrangements you agree on.

Before moving in

Discuss how you each use the space on an ordinary weekday. Compare quiet hours, working-from-home needs, and the areas you expect to share. Make sure everyone understands the existing lease and bills; this guide does not replace those documents or provide legal advice.

  • Which spaces are shared, and which are private?
  • When do you usually sleep, work, cook, or take calls?
  • What notice would you like before an overnight guest visits?
  • How will you communicate a temporary change in routine?

Make chores specific

“Keep things clean” can mean different things to different people. Choose a few concrete tasks and decide how often they need attention. A short rota can be easier to follow than repeated reminders, but only if everyone agrees the tasks are realistic.

  • Who handles bins, shared surfaces, and restocking common supplies?
  • What should happen to dishes after cooking?
  • Which items are shared, and which require asking first?
  • How will you handle a week when someone is away?

After the first week

Have a brief check-in before a small annoyance becomes a routine. Describe an observable situation and a possible change: “Could we keep calls out of the living room after ten?” is easier to act on than a broad judgment about consideration.

Keep the discussion separate from a quiz result

A recreational personality quiz cannot decide who is the better roommate or explain another person's motives. If you want a shared activity after the practical conversation, choose a game together. A trivia duel or a game for friends can be a pleasant way to spend time without using labels to settle household decisions.
