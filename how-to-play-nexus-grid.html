How to Play Nexus Grid

A practical guide to grouping words, spotting red herrings, and working through the final connection.

Nexus Grid asks you to find four groups of four related words. All sixteen words are visible, but a word may suggest more than one connection. Your job is to work out which grouping uses the whole grid consistently.

Start with a candidate, not a click

Read every word before submitting a group. Look for a connection you can explain in a short phrase: a set of objects, a shared word ending, a familiar expression, or a category. Select four candidates and ask whether all four fit equally well. Three strong matches and a fourth that only half fits are a reason to pause.

Keep an eye on the leftovers

A tempting group may use a word needed somewhere else. Before committing, check what the remaining words could form. If a choice leaves an obvious group with only three members, try swapping an overlapping word. The puzzle is solved as a complete arrangement, not as four unrelated guesses.

Make each attempt useful

If a group is rejected, do not immediately submit nearly the same set. Reconsider the category itself. Is it too broad? Could one word have a second meaning? Does the connection rely on how a word sounds rather than what it means? Keep a rejected idea in mind without letting it dominate the rest of the puzzle.

When you get stuck

Read the remaining words aloud or rearrange them if the game offers a shuffle. A new order can break the visual grouping you have been assuming. Taking a short break can help too. The goal is an enjoyable deduction, so there is no benefit to rushing a guess just to keep moving.

Ready to try? Play Nexus Grid. If you prefer a single hidden sequence with direct feedback after each guess, try Fusion Core next.
