A Practical Guide to Word Ladders

Work backward, preserve useful letters, and find a path between two words.

A word ladder connects a starting word to a target word by changing one letter at each step. Each intermediate step must also be a word accepted by the game. The length stays the same: changing COLD to CORD is one move; adding a letter is not.

Look at the letters that differ

Compare the start and target positions. Every different position needs at least one change, which gives you a useful lower bound. A direct replacement may not form a word, so the actual path can be longer. Do not assume the shortest-looking idea is the only valid route.

Work from both ends

If you cannot find a useful neighbor of the starting word, try changing one letter of the target. A familiar word near the target can give you an intermediate goal. You can keep one or two possible paths in mind and look for a word that joins them.

Use flexible letter patterns

Some words have many common neighbors; others leave you with very few. A small detour through a flexible pattern can be more productive than a direct-looking move into a dead end. Read each candidate as a whole word before submitting it rather than changing letters at random.

Learn from the dictionary feedback

A rejected word may be outside the game's dictionary even if you have seen it elsewhere. Proper names, abbreviations, and specialist terms are often excluded. Try an everyday alternative. If a common word is consistently rejected, send the word and the puzzle details through the correction page.
