A Better Game Break at Work

Choose a short, optional game that fits the time, the group, and the devices available.

A short game at work can be an enjoyable shared break. It should be optional, easy to join, and small enough to fit the time you actually have. It is not a treatment for workload problems or a way to assess employees.

Start with a clear invitation

Say how long you expect to play and make it easy to decline. A five-minute round before a social meeting feels different from an unexpected competition during a deadline. Avoid asking people to explain why they would rather sit it out.

Choose a format that suits the group

For a shared screen and individual phones, use a hosted trivia room. For two people on a break, a same-device duel takes less setup. If people are working independently, share a link to the Daily Ten and let them compare results later.

A broad topic is usually easier to join than a specialist fandom. If the group wants a themed round, let different people choose the topic in turn. Keep the first round unscored if everyone is learning the controls.

Set a stopping point

Agree on one round or a fixed end time before starting. A game that overruns can make a break feel like another obligation. The host can read the answer, leave room for a brief comment, and move on without turning every question into a debate.

Keep results out of performance discussions

Trivia scores reflect the questions, the topic, and that particular round. They should not be used as a ranking of colleagues' ability or as an employee wellness measure. Avoid collecting personal quiz results or using a reflection category to assign roles at work.

If the real issue is workload

A game can be a pleasant activity, but it cannot resolve an overloaded calendar, unclear priorities, or a difficult working arrangement. Keep those conversations separate from the quiz. For a recreational check-in about preferred routines, try Workday Energy; its result is a prompt, not an assessment.

For a longer social event, see Host a Better Quiz Night.
